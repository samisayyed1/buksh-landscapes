Walls that hold back soil safely, create usable levels and frame your garden, built to stay straight for decades.

Hardscape

Built right, walls stay straight. Built wrong, they lean.

Retaining walls turn slopes into usable space, frame beds and add structure to a yard. Whether in precast block or natural armour stone, a well-built wall should hold its line for decades and complement the rest of your landscape.

Walls fail when water builds up behind them or the first course sits on a weak base. We build every wall on a compacted base with drainage aggregate behind it, so pressure has somewhere to go and the wall stays plumb winter after winter.

What's included

  • Segmental block and armour stone walls
  • Compacted base under the first course
  • Drainage aggregate behind the wall
  • Terraced beds and raised planters
  • Steps built into slopes
  • 2-year workmanship coverage

Best forSloped lots, walkout basements, and yards that need more flat space.

How we build it

Done properly, in the right order.

  1. 01

    Excavate

    We dig a trench for the base and set the wall's line and height to suit the slope.

  2. 02

    Base course

    A compacted base and a perfectly level first course: everything above depends on it.

  3. 03

    Build and drain

    Courses go up with drainage aggregate and backfill compacted behind them as we go.

  4. 04

    Cap and finish

    Caps are set and the soil and planting finished to the wall.

Retaining walls questions

Straight answers.

Something we didn't cover? Call 780-940-8283, 9 am to 5 pm, 7 days a week.

Block or armour stone?

Block gives clean, uniform lines and fits modern homes. Armour stone is natural, massive and suits larger slopes and acreages. We'll show you both.

Why do retaining walls lean?

Usually water pressure behind the wall or a weak first course. Drainage aggregate and a compacted base prevent both.

Do I need a permit for a retaining wall?

Taller walls can need engineering and approval. We'll tell you at the site visit if yours does.
